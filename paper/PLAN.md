# Paper plan

## Target

ISMB 2027, proceedings track, published in *Bioinformatics*. The 2026 call opened in November 2025 and closed on
20 January 2026, so the 2027 deadline is expected in mid to late January 2027 (to confirm when the call is posted, with the
page limit and template). Fallback: RECOMB 2027; journal route: *Nature Communications* or *mSystems*, where the closest
prior work (gRodon, Phydon) appeared.

Why ISMB: the contribution is a computational method plus an evaluation of how well it transfers to uncultured genomes,
with a biological finding attached. That is the core of ISMB's proceedings track. A machine-learning venue would expect a
new learning method, which this paper does not claim.

## Rules for the whole paper

1. Every number in the text, tables and figures comes from `paper/numbers.tex` or a figure script, generated from the
   result files by `paper/scripts/make_numbers.py`. No number is typed by hand. `numbers_sources.csv` says where each comes
   from.
2. Every claim is in `paper/CLAIMS.md` with its evidence. A claim without evidence is cut, not softened.
3. Every reference is checked against Crossref (title, first author, year, journal) before it enters `refs.bib`.
4. Plain, direct sentences, written for a computational biologist. No em dashes. No hype words ("novel", "powerful",
   "state of the art") unless a number backs them, and then the number is said instead.
5. Negative results stay in: the low-energy test, the slow-growth gains that disappeared with clean labels, the syntrophy
   marker. They are part of what makes the evaluation credible.
6. Honest comparisons only: the released gRodon and Phydon, run on our genes and folds, with their input advantage stated.
